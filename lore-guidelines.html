Worldbuilding & Continuity

Lore Guidelines

Blood Without Honor is a setting of inherited power, political terror, sacred oaths, and consequence.

The Realm is not a place where characters move through history untouched. Every House, title, betrayal, feud, war, and act of mercy contributes to a shared continuity. The world survives by memory, reputation, and the weight of what came before.

These guidelines define how lore should be treated: as a living record shaped by actions, judged by consequence, and protected by the proper authority.

I. The Nature of the Realm

The Realm is shaped by established canon, recorded history, and the consequences of every action taken within it. Every character, House, and event contributes to a shared historical record.

What qualifies as canon?

Canon consists of established lore, recognized historical precedent, lawful decrees, and the traditions governing the Realm.

Canon-based action

Actions must be supported by recorded history, recognized House tradition, lawful standing, or an accepted IC process.

What does not qualify?

OOC coordination, contradictions of established lore, bypassing House restrictions, or ignoring RP limitations do not create canon.

Why actions matter: Succession, war, House standing, region control, political alliances, and historical memory all depend on what characters do. The Realm remembers.

II. Characters & Immersion

Lore characters must remain grounded in their origins, backgrounds, and the realities of the setting. Personalities, relationships, and ambitions are permitted when they remain believable in context.

Character realism

Powergaming, unjustified additions, and empty status-grabbing are prohibited. Extraordinary qualities must be justified.

In-character conduct

During official RP, players are expected to remain IC and act according to their character's knowledge, position, and constraints.

Immersion-breaking acts

Unwarranted violence, OOC interference, impossible knowledge, and random disregard for setting logic all damage the continuity of the Realm.

“A character is not a weapon. A character is a history that has not yet finished writing itself.”

III. Knowledge & Power

Metagaming is the use of knowledge a character would not reasonably possess. That rule is essential to preserving immersion, threats, and the weight of uncertainty.

Legitimate IC sources

Decrees, ravens, messengers, public proclamations, and direct IC channels are valid sources of information.

What is not valid?

Any information the character could not reasonably know, including OOC coordination or hidden leaks, remains inaccessible IC.

Public decrees

Public decrees are an exception, because they are formally announced to the wider Realm and may thus become common knowledge.

Rule: If a message or raven was not sent to a character, that character cannot act on its contents without legitimate IC access.

IV. Events & Story Progression

Lore events drive the history of the Realm. They allow Houses, factions, and characters to shape the world, gain influence, face consequences, and leave their mark on history.

What is a lore event?

Any IC scenario that meaningfully affects the status, power, influence, or historical direction of a House, faction, region, or the Realm.

Examples

Wars, sieges, coronations, feasts, marriages, political councils, raids, treaties, and major political decisions.

Recognition

All major events must occur through the proper IC channels and remain consistent with established lore and rule structures.

Event type Requirements
Political conflict Must be lawful, justified, and contextually plausible.
Military action Must respect war rules, territory, and House limits.
Major ceremony Must be recognized as a formal world event and remain within setting logic.
Historical consequence Only official, recognized IC actions become historical canon.

V. Combat, Injury & Death

Combat has consequences. A blade is not a mere cosmetic flourish; it alters the body, the standing, and the fate of a House. The Realm is not a place where violence is clean or painless.

Injuries

Characters are expected to roleplay injuries realistically according to their severity.

Permanent consequences

Grievous wounds may affect combat ability, officeholdings, inheritance, political standing, and public image.

Perma-death

Death is permanent unless a specific approved mechanism permits otherwise. Death must be recognized by lawful IC process.

Important: Severe injuries may alter a character's capacity and legitimacy. They are not merely temporary nuisances.

VI. Prison, Exile & Justice

Capture, imprisonment, exile, and execution are legitimate consequences of conflict and political action. The law is not an ornament. It is a weapon, a shield, and a burden.

Prison

A character may be imprisoned after lawful capture through war, political action, or recognized conflict.

Ransom

Captured persons may be ransomed, exchanged, or negotiated through lawful IC processes.

Trial

Knights and higher-status characters may request trial by combat, trial of the Seven, or other recognized legal proceedings.

Escape

Escapes must remain difficult, plausible, and supported by circumstance. They are not automatic outcomes.

Exile

House exile or placement in exile may be used either in place of death or as a political consequence.

Execution

Serious offenses may result in execution when lawful authority and appropriate process exist.

VII. Houses, Families & Bloodlines

Houses form the foundation of the Realm's political and social structure. Their influence is shaped by ambition, standing, bloodline, and the limitations established by the Realm itself.

House Type Core Rule
Minor House Limited authority, limited retainers, strict House caps, and a narrower political footprint.
Paramount House Greater territorial power, stronger military presence, and wider political influence.
Crown House Exclusive royal legitimacy and sovereignty, with recognized succession and authority.
Wandering Court Mobility and independence, but reduced structure, authority, and militarized backing.
Important: House caps and Dynasty limits exist to prevent abuse, excessive power inflation, or artificial political growth. They are not optional suggestions.

VIII. Politics

The Realm is a world where bloodlines, titles, and ambitions constantly intersect. Political life is not a game of decoration; it is a struggle for legitimacy, influence, succession, and survival.

Crown House dissolution

If a Crown House dissolves, the Throne becomes vacant and the Realm enters an interregnum unless a lawful successor is secured.

Interregnum

During a vacancy, no crowned monarch holds lawful authority. Paramount Houses retain regional power, not royal authority.

Eligible claimants

Potential claimants may include surviving dynasty members, lawful heirs, or Houses meeting the proper requirements for a Throne war.

“No succession is sacred if it is not sustained by law, legitimacy, and force.”

IX. Marriage, Alliances & Scandal

Marriage and alliance are not decorative. They are political instruments, social contracts, and sometimes weapons. They must be treated with the seriousness they deserve.

Political marriages

Valid marriages must be publicly declared, acknowledged by the relevant Houses, and remain within lawful House and Dynasty constraints.

Reputation

Reputation functions as a resource. It may be won, lost, or weaponized. A House's standing is not merely symbolic; it changes what others believe is possible.

Scandal & rumor

Rumor is not the same as fact. A rumor may damage a House or character's image, but it remains just a rumor until recognized by evidence, law, or official record.

Scandal becomes canon

A scandal becomes significant when the public, a House, or the proper authority recognizes it as valid and consequential.

Core principle: Reputation matters. A ruined name, a broken oath, or a public betrayal can be more dangerous than a blade.

X. Messaging & Decrees

Ravens and decrees are the lifeblood of political continuity. They are the official channels by which news, law, and command travel across the Realm.

Ravens

Ravens are the recognized form of long-distance communication. They are binding when properly delivered and acknowledged.

Proclamations

Noble decrees and proclamations are the highest form of political declaration and may shape succession, war, title transfer, and law.

Public vs. private declarations

Public declarations may alter legitimacy, dynastic standing, or political claims. Private declarations may hold weight only if properly acknowledged.

XI. Retcons & Historical Record

History within the Realm is persistent and consequential. The world remembers what was law, what was done, and what was lost.

When corrections are valid

A retcon may be used to correct genuine errors, false assumptions, or administrative mistakes that violate continuity.

When corrections are not valid

A retcon may not erase lawful wars, valid executions, rightful enthronements, or meaningful consequences simply because a player dislikes the outcome.

Finality

Once recognized by proper authority, major events become the official record of the Realm and remain part of its memory.

XII. Supernatural & Mystical Lore

The Realm may contain whispers of the supernatural, ancient fear, and unresolved mysteries. However, the supernatural is not a casual power system. It is rare, dangerous, and carefully controlled.

Existence

Elements such as ancient prophecy, unusual bloodline claims, divine omen, or forbidden lore may exist within the setting.

Approval requirement

Any supernatural element introduced into active play must be approved by the Administration before it may be recognized as canon.

Consequences

Supernatural power is not mundane. It changes the balance of nations, politics, and myth. Its use must be rare and meaningful.

No spontaneous claim

Characters may not simply declare dragon blood, magical power, or mystical lineage without evidence, approval, and proper setting justification.

Rule: The supernatural may exist in the Realm, but it must be rare, intentional, and administratively validated before it becomes true in play.

XIII. Lore Department & Administration

The Lore Department and the Administration serve distinct but connected functions. Together they preserve continuity, interpret the rules of the setting, and ensure that the Realm remains coherent.

Lore Department

The Lore Department is responsible for maintaining historical consistency, preserving canon, and determining whether a claim, event, or lineage fits established lore.

Administration

The Administration is the final authority over the rules, interpretation, and enforcement of all Realm matters. Its decisions are binding.

Authority split

The Lore Department reviews continuity and world consistency. The Administration resolves disputes, enforces the rules, and approves exceptional cases.

Agency Role Power
Lore Department Historic continuity, canon tracking, lore interpretation Advisory and interpretive authority
Administration Final rule enforcement, dispute settlement, exceptional decisions Final authority
Final authority: The Administration has ultimate power to decide final rulings, resolve disputes, enforce continuity, and determine the legitimacy of exceptions. The Lore Department advises and interprets, but the Administration decides.
“History is not changed by the weak. It is corrected only when the strong and the lawful agree there was a mistake.”

Final Principle

The Realm is not a place for convenience. It is a place for consequence.

Every title carries weight. Every oath can be broken. Every bloodline can be challenged. Every war can change the shape of the world. And every action leaves behind a scar in history, whether it is one the world remembers with reverence, with fear, or with hatred.
